Tools I have published

I direct the building of small tools that check what people already produce, from meeting transcripts to price lists. For each tool I decide which real files it is measured against, and that the results which do not flatter it are published beside the rest. AI agents write the code and the documentation.

  • Follow Through finds the commitments people make out loud in a meeting transcript and tracks each one until somebody closes it. Reads English and Hinglish, because most meetings in India are not held in one language.
  • Show Your Work finds the numbers in a spreadsheet that nobody can explain: a formula someone typed over, a total that stops one row short, a cell doing something different from its column.
  • Before You Send reads a PDF and reports what is still inside it that you may not mean to send: text under a box that was never removed, an earlier draft, a name in the properties.
  • Adds Up reads a price list and reports the arithmetic in it that contradicts itself: a volume tier where buying more costs more per unit, the same item priced two different ways, a stated discount that does not reconcile with the prices beside it. It never says whether the pricing is right.
  • On The List reads a cosmetic ingredient list and reports what the EU's official annexes say about the ingredients on it: a name on the prohibited list, a colour printed out of position, the same ingredient entered twice. It reports a match as a match, never as a verdict on the product.
  • Says On The Tin finds where a cosmetic label contradicts itself: a free-from claim on the front, and an ingredient on the back that breaks it. Paraben-free over a list containing methylparaben, vegan over carmine. It never says a product is compliant or clean.
  • On Notice reads a cosmetic ingredient list and reports which ingredients on it are named in an EU rule that carries a date still ahead of it. The others check what is wrong today; reformulation takes the better part of a year, so the question that costs money is what becomes wrong while the stock is still on the shelf. It reports dates, never a verdict on whether a product may be sold.
  • Repo Scout reports checkable evidence about a public code repository, and reports what it could not check as unknown rather than as a clean bill of health.
